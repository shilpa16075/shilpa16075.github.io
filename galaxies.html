🌌 Milky Way Galaxy

The Milky Way is our home galaxy — a massive spiral galaxy containing over 100 billion stars, including our Sun. It stretches across 100,000 light-years and features bright arms filled with gas, dust, and nebulae. The Milky Way has a supermassive black hole, Sagittarius A*, at its center that holds everything together through its immense gravity.

🌠 Andromeda Galaxy (M31)

The Andromeda Galaxy is the closest spiral galaxy to the Milky Way, located about 2.5 million light-years away. It is nearly twice as large as the Milky Way and is on a slow collision course with it. Scientists predict that both galaxies will merge in about 4 billion years to form a giant elliptical galaxy.

✨ Triangulum Galaxy (M33)

The Triangulum Galaxy is a small spiral galaxy and the third-largest member of our Local Group, after the Milky Way and Andromeda. It is about 3 million light-years away and has well-defined spiral arms with young, bright blue stars — making it a hub of star formation.

🌪️ Whirlpool Galaxy (M51)

The Whirlpool Galaxy is one of the most stunning examples of a spiral galaxy, with two perfectly symmetrical arms. Located 23 million light-years away, its structure shows how gravity interacts between galaxies. A smaller companion galaxy can be seen tugging on one of its arms, creating a breathtaking cosmic dance.

🌈 Sombrero Galaxy (M104)

The Sombrero Galaxy is famous for its bright central bulge and wide, dark dust ring that makes it look like a cosmic hat. It lies about 31 million light-years away and has a massive black hole at its center, billions of times the mass of the Sun. Its beauty and symmetry make it a favorite target for astronomers and photographers alike.
